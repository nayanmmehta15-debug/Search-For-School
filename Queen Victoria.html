Imagine waking up one morning as an ordinary eighteen-year-old and being told that you are now the queen of the whole country. That is exactly what happened to Queen Victoria. She went on to reign for more than sixty-three years, which was longer than any British monarch before her. Her time on the throne was so remarkable that we still call it the Victorian era today. In this page, I am going to tell you all about her extraordinary life, from her lonely childhood to her magnificent funeral.

A Lonely Childhood

Victoria was born on 24 May 1819 at Kensington Palace in London. Her father, Prince Edward, Duke of Kent, died when Victoria was less than a year old.

Victoria was raised mainly by her mother, the Duchess of Kent. Her childhood was very controlled and she was rarely allowed to be alone.

Victoria's childhood was unusual because everyone knew that she might one day become queen. She was taught history, languages, music, drawing and other subjects that would prepare her for her future role.

Becoming Queen

Victoria's uncle, King William IV, died on 20 June 1837. Victoria was only eighteen years old.

She became Queen of the United Kingdom and began one of the longest reigns in British history.

Her coronation took place on 28 June 1838 at Westminster Abbey.

Victoria quickly became a powerful symbol of Britain's changing nineteenth-century society.

Prince Albert

Victoria married her cousin, Prince Albert of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha, in 1840.

They eventually had nine children. Their children married into several European royal families, helping create a large network of royal relatives.

Albert became an important adviser to Victoria. He was interested in science, technology, education and the arts.

Britain Changes

During Victoria's reign, Britain changed dramatically. The Industrial Revolution transformed the country.

Factories became more important, cities grew rapidly and new machines changed the way people worked and travelled.

Britain's railway network expanded enormously. Steam trains made it much easier for people and goods to travel across the country.

New inventions and discoveries also changed everyday life.

The Great Exhibition

One of the most famous events of Victoria's reign was the Great Exhibition of 1851.

It was held inside the enormous Crystal Palace in London's Hyde Park.

The exhibition showed inventions, machinery, artwork and products from Britain and countries around the world.

Prince Albert played an important role in organising the exhibition.

The British Empire

During Victoria's reign, the British Empire expanded and changed. Britain controlled territories in many parts of the world.

In 1876, Victoria was given the title Empress of India.

The empire brought Britain enormous power, but imperial rule also involved conflict, inequality and the loss of control by people in territories ruled by Britain.

Understanding the Victorian period therefore means studying both Britain's achievements and the difficult consequences of empire.

A Devastating Loss

In 1861, Prince Albert became seriously ill and died at Windsor Castle.

Victoria was devastated by his death. She spent many years mourning him and wore black clothing for much of the rest of her life.

Although she withdrew from some public life for a period, she eventually returned to many of her royal duties.

The Victorian Era

Victoria's name became connected with an entire era. The Victorian era lasted from her accession in 1837 until her death in 1901.

Victorian Britain saw major developments in science, engineering, literature, education and transport.

Famous writers such as Charles Dickens, George Eliot and Thomas Hardy produced important works during the period.

Victoria's Jubilees

Victoria's long reign meant that she celebrated several major anniversaries of becoming queen.

Her Golden Jubilee took place in 1887, marking fifty years on the throne.

Her Diamond Jubilee took place in 1897, marking sixty years as queen.

These celebrations showed how famous and important Victoria had become to many people in Britain and across the empire.

The End of an Era

Queen Victoria died on 22 January 1901 at Osborne House on the Isle of Wight.

She had been queen for 63 years and 7 months.

Her death marked the end of the Victorian era. Her son Edward became King Edward VII.

Victoria's funeral was a huge national event watched by enormous crowds.

Victoria's Legacy

Queen Victoria remains one of the most famous monarchs in British history.

Her reign saw enormous changes in technology, transport, science, industry and society.

The period also had serious problems, including poverty, inequality and the effects of Britain's expanding empire.

That is why studying Victoria is useful: it helps us understand both the achievements and the problems of nineteenth-century Britain.
